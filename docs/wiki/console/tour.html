A tour of canopy console

Every screen of canopy console, what it shows, and the key to get there.

Everything in canopy console lives in one window with ten tabs. Each tab is a screen, usually with a list on the left and details on the right. This page shows every screen, what it is for, and the key that gets you there. On a narrow terminal the tab names shorten to Log, Refs, Repos, PRs and CI.

The examples come from a small demo repository. Run canopy inside any git repository to see your own.

Moving around#

KeyWhat it does
1 to 9, 0Jump to a tab: Home, Changes, History, Branches, Stash, Workspace, Reflog, Pull requests, Issues, Actions.
tab and ⇧tabNext and previous tab.
j k or ↓ ↑Move down and up a list. g and G jump to the top and bottom.
↵ and escOpen the selected item, and go back.
?Every key that works on the screen you are on.
:The command palette: type a few letters of any action and press ↵. ⌃P and ⌃K open it too.
TShow or hide teach mode, the footer that shows the git command behind each action.
⌃TCycle through the themes.
qQuit.

The bar at the bottom always lists the most useful keys for the screen you are on. Actions that work everywhere have the same key on every tab: c commit, p pull, P push, f fetch, z undo and S stash.

Home (1)#

Where you are and what to do next. The top panel shows the branch, what it tracks, how far ahead or behind it is, and counts of staged, changed, conflicted and stashed files. Next steps turns that into plain advice with the key to press: "2 file(s) staged and ready. Press c to commit." Recent commits with a graph are below. On a wide terminal, a GitHub card on the right shows the pull request and checks for your branch.

Changes (2)#

Everything you have edited, in three groups: Conflicts, Changes and Staged. The right side shows the diff of the selected file.

KeyAction
spaceStage or unstage the file.
aStage or unstage everything.
↵Open the diff to stage single lines or hunks.
cCommit what is staged.
AAmend the last commit.
dDiscard your edits to the file (asks first, and cannot be undone).
eOpen the file in your editor.
iAdd the file to .gitignore.
L and BHistory of the file, and blame.

History (3)#

Every commit, newest first, with a branch graph, the short hash, message, author and age. The commit's changes show on the right; ↵ moves into them and h moves back. / searches. From here you can act on a commit:

KeyAction
spaceCheck out the commit.
nStart a new branch at the commit.
TTag the commit.
C and tCherry-pick the commit, and revert it.
rReset the branch to the commit (a menu asks how).
iInteractive rebase from the commit.
FFold staged changes into the commit as a fixup.
yCopy the hash.

Branches (4)#

Local and remote branches with their ahead and behind counts. ] and [ switch the list between Branches, Tags, Remotes, Worktrees and Submodules.

KeyAction
space or ↵Check out the branch.
nNew branch.
M and rMerge the selected branch into the current one, and rebase the current one onto it.
R and uRename, and set the upstream.
dDelete.

Actions (0)#

CI runs for your branch, updating live. e shows what went wrong, L the whole log, R re-runs the failed jobs and A hands a failure to your AI assistant. ] switches to Releases. Your GitHub inbox is not a tab: ⌥N opens Notifications from anywhere.

The command palette#

Forgot a key? Press : and type what you want to do, such as "stash" or "tag". The palette lists the matching actions for the screen you are on, with their keys, and ↵ runs the one you select. Your own custom commands from the config are in it too.
